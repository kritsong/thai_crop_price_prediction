# Exploratory Data Analysis — Thai Crop Price Dataset (Full Redo, 2026-07-14)

This report documents a from-scratch re-execution of the exploratory data analysis for the crop price forecasting manuscript. Every statistic was recomputed directly from the raw JSON files in `d:/new_crop_data/historical_data_2018/` using the exact preprocessing of `src/data/loader.py` (zero-to-NaN casting, rolling 30-day IQR outlier removal, forward/backward fill, Monday–Friday business-day skeleton alignment for 2018-01-03 to 2025-12-30), with the volatility gate bypassed so that all active series are profiled. Per-product results are in `eda_product_summary.csv`; the four manuscript figures (`acf_pacf.png`, `price_trend.png`, `stationarity_by_group.png`, `correlation_heatmap.png`) were regenerated by the same run.

## 2. Liquidity and the Volatility Thresholds

The move rate (share of business days with a non-zero price change, computed on cleaned maximum prices, as in the loader) has a **median of only 2.45%** across active series — half the dataset barely reprices. The two documented thresholds reproduce exactly:

| Threshold | Products retained |
|---|---|
| ≥ 1% move rate (paper's training/evaluation universe) | **404** |
| ≥ 5% move rate (draft-phase universe, legacy reference suite) | **213** |

This confirms the provenance of both universes from the raw data alone.

## 3. Unit-Root Structure (ADF, levels, p < 0.05)

Across all 635 active series, only **30.9%** of minimum-price and **28.8%** of maximum-price series reject the unit root — the dataset is predominantly non-stationary in levels, consistent with near-random-walk behavior.

**By market category** (manuscript Table 2, updated to these reproduced values):

| Category | n | Stationary min price | Stationary max price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retail | 269 | 35.7% | 33.1% |
| Wholesale | 366 | 27.3% | 25.7% |

Retail series are more mean-reverting than wholesale ones (price stickiness at the consumer end), and minimum prices slightly more than maximum prices. Note: these rates are ~1pp below the previous EDA's figures (36.8/34.6/28.1/26.0) — attributable to ADF implementation/version differences; the qualitative ordering is unchanged and the manuscript now carries the reproduced values.

**By product group**, stationarity is extremely heterogeneous (see `stationarity_by_group.png`): Fresh Vegetables **82%**, Fruits **72%**, Aquatic & Marine 59/46% — short cultivation and spoilage cycles force mean reversion — versus near-total non-stationarity for storable and administered products: Wholesale/Retail Rice 0–6%, Oil Crops 6%, Meat & Poultry 4–8%, Feed 0%.

## 4. Autocorrelation Structure

Averaging the ACF and PACF in levels within each product group and plotting one panel per group (`acf_pacf.png`; twelve groups have active series — Miscellaneous/Unknown has none) shows the unit-root signature is **universal, not cherry-picked**: in every group the PACF collapses to a single dominant spike at lag 1 (0.97–0.999) and is negligible thereafter — the series are first-order autoregressive with a near-unit root, so once yesterday's price is known, earlier history adds essentially nothing. The groups differ only in the *rate* of ACF decay, ranked by 40-day persistence:

| Group | n | ACF at lag 40 | | Group | n | ACF at lag 40 |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| Feed & Raw Materials | 11 | 0.92 | | Field Crops | 35 | 0.84 |
| Wholesale Rice & Bags | 48 | 0.87 | | Organic Veg & Fruits | 25 | 0.78 |
| Oil Crops & Oils | 86 | 0.87 | | Food Crops | 77 | 0.70 |
| Retail Rice | 9 | 0.87 | | Aquatic & Marine | 34 | 0.59 |
| Wholesale Rice to Retailers | 10 | 0.87 | | Fruits | 62 | 0.52 |
| Meat & Poultry | 94 | 0.84 | | Fresh Vegetables | 89 | 0.39 |

Storability and administered pricing — not market size — govern persistence: feed, rice in every form, and oil crops barely decay, while perishables mean-revert. The cross-group mean remains elevated at 0.75. This is the canonical AR(1)-with-unit-root pattern — tomorrow's price is yesterday's price plus noise — and it explains simultaneously why (i) the Lag-1 persistence baseline is nearly unbeatable at short horizons and (ii) ARIMA fitted per product collapses onto that same baseline (Table 3 of the manuscript: identical MAE to four significant figures). The decay-rate spread also foreshadows the stationarity gradient of Section 3.

## 5. Long-Term Trends

Indexed to 2018 = 100, the six largest groups show strongly heterogeneous drift (`price_trend.png`): Oil Crops & Oils peaked near +80% in 2022 before a second run-up in 2025; Meat & Poultry rose ~50–65% with a 2022 peak; Wholesale Rice & Bags is the outlier, ending 2025 *at or below* its 2018 level after a sustained 2024–25 decline. Common macro shocks (the 2022 spike) coexist with group-specific regimes — shared structure for a pooled model, entity-specific levels for static embeddings.

## 6. Cross-Product Co-Movement

**Dataset-wide (all 81,406 pairs of the 404-crop modelling universe)** — the numbers that should be quoted:

| | mean r | median | share > 0.5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Levels, all pairs | **0.180** | 0.176 | 14.3% |
| Levels, within group | 0.357 | — | — |
| Levels, across groups | 0.152 | — | — |
| Returns, all pairs | **0.012** | 0.000 | 1.0% |
| Returns, within group | 0.091 | — | — |
| Returns, across groups | −0.001 | — | — |

Broad market integration is therefore **weak**, and in daily returns essentially absent.

**The 40 most liquid series** (`correlation_heatmap.png`) are the extreme, unrepresentative corner of this distribution — mean r = 0.574 in levels, 66% of pairs above 0.5 — because that subset is dominated by internationally traded rubber, palm, cassava and grain contracts. Decomposed:

| Among the 40 most liquid | mean r (levels) | mean r (returns) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Within commodity family | **0.940** | 0.377 |
| Across families | 0.481 | 0.037 |

The within-family figure is near-mechanical rather than economic: the rubber block is one USS3/RSS3 sheet grade quoted at three provinces and two market levels; the palm block is crude/RBD/olein/stearin variants of a single oil complex plus fresh fruit bunches priced per province. **Caution: an earlier version of this report quoted the 0.57 / 66% / 0.11 figures as dataset-wide. They are not — they describe this liquid subset only.**

The analytically important findings are therefore: (i) there is almost no cross-sectional signal to exploit — pooling cannot be justified by correlated prices, and this is precisely why no pooled model beats persistence at short horizons; a global model instead shares the *functional form* of the dynamics and transfers among the related series that do co-move; and (ii) what integration exists lives in slow trends, not daily shocks (returns ≈ 0 across groups), which is why the pooled advantage materialises only as the horizon lengthens — exactly the horizon profile the manuscript's results exhibit.

## 7. Implications for Modeling

1. **Near-random-walk levels** ⇒ persistence is the correct short-horizon benchmark and is genuinely hard to beat; differencing or drift-robust architectures are required to avoid spurious fits.
2. **Low move rates** ⇒ volatility filtering is essential; the 1% threshold (404 crops) balances coverage against degenerate step-function series.
3. **Heterogeneous stationarity and drift by group** ⇒ entity/group embeddings are necessary; one shared dynamic will not fit vegetables and rice simultaneously.
4. **Trend-level (not return-level) co-movement** ⇒ cross-product pooling pays at long horizons; short-horizon gains should not be expected from pooling alone.
5. **4.6% zero-artifacts and heavy tails** ⇒ zero-casting plus rolling-IQR cleanup before any model sees the data.
